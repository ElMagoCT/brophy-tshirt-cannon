One board, WiFi on it

your question: a stronger ESP32?

Pick: an ESP32-P4 board with an ESP32-C5 on the same PCB (Wireless Tag WTDKP4C5-S1, $29.29). It replaces the STM32, the separate C5 bridge and the UART wire between them. The P4 runs everything; the C5 next to it is the radio, wired on the board.

  • WiFi: WiFi 6 on 2.4 and 5 GHz, so the dashboard keeps its 5 GHz access point out of the crowded gym band.
  • Camera: a 2-lane MIPI camera port and a hardware H.264/JPEG encoder: a $10–20 camera module streams from this board.
  • Lidar: a spare UART (LD06/LD19 style) or I2C (TF-Luna) is all it needs.
  • LEDs: 16 parallel output lanes. 1,680 LEDs on one line is about 50 ms a frame; split over 8 lanes it's about 6 ms.
  • Pressure: the P4's ADC is far better than older ESP32s, but the transducers still go through an ADS1115 ($7.99), the settled call. The P4's ADC reads the pot and battery volts.

Bluetooth music needs one small extra board. Phones stream music over Bluetooth Classic (A2DP). Only the original ESP32 and the brand-new S31 have it; the C5, C6, S3 and P4 are Bluetooth LE only. So a $8.99 ESP32 board acts as the phone's speaker and sends the audio over I2S into the P4. The P4 mixes the horn and effects over it and sends volume and pause back over a UART. The dashboard stays in charge; if that board dies, only phone music stops.

Catches: the board is new (3 sold), and its camera and LED drivers want ESP-IDF; Arduino support for the P4 is partial.

What got simpler

rev D.1 → D.2
  • The PWM gate is gone. The contactor is the cut-off, as you said. Its coil runs through the rear E-stop, the safety node and the main controller in series, so any one of them kills drive, valves, LEDs, audio and fog at once.
  • One controller instead of two. No STM32, no bridge board, no UART protocol between them to write and debug.
  • The safety node has one job: it holds the contactor in while the remote's heartbeat and the main controller's heartbeat are both alive. It's your spare ESP32-S3, so it costs nothing, and because it's separate a crashed main board can't keep the robot powered.
  • Valves keep a hardware arm relay. It's fed from the PDH, so it dies with the contactor, and the rear E-stop's second contact is in its coil. A stuck MOSFET still can't fire a disarmed gun.
  • MOSFET modules trigger from 3.3 V, so they hang straight off the controller. The 74AHCT125s now only level-shift the LED data.
